Methodology

Data source

NISR Seasonal Agricultural Survey (SAS) 2024, Annual Report and Annual Tables: district-level cultivated area, harvested area, yield and production by crop for Seasons A, B and C, plus district shares of farmers using improved seed, fertilizer, pesticide, irrigation, mechanization and erosion protection. The platform currently runs on these published tables. We checked that district totals add up to the national figures NISR publishes (for example maize cultivated area in Season A, 249,435 ha).

Data processing

A script reads the NISR workbook, keeps the 30 districts and 15 individual crops (group totals such as “Cereals” are excluded to avoid double counting), and saves one clean file. Cells with no cultivation are left out. Nothing is estimated or filled in.

Yield definition

Following the SAS report, yield (kg/ha) = crop production (kg) / crop area (ha). The district yields on this site are NISR’s own. Where we combine districts, yield is total production divided by total harvested area. Area is GPS-measured; production is reported by farmers, so yields carry reporting error. Perennial crops such as cassava and banana have a much smaller harvested area than cultivated area.

Machine learning

Status: the prediction model needs plot-level survey microdata, which is not loaded yet, so no model results are shown. Planned design: target: yield. Features: only pre-harvest variables (location, crop, season, plot area, seed, fertilizer, pesticide, erosion, mechanization, irrigation). Harvest, sales, storage and loss quantities are excluded to prevent data leakage. Models: Linear Regression, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting; metrics: MAE, RMSE, R².

Risk methodology

Rule-based, not ML. Index = observed production / expected production, where expected = harvested area × national yield of the same crop and season. An index of 1.0 means the district performs at the national level for its crop mix. HIGH risk: index below 0.90; MEDIUM: 0.90 to 0.99; LOW: 1.00 or above. The thresholds are provisional. The “observed differences” listed for a district compare its input use and erosion with the district average; they are associations, not causes.

Limitations

  • Observational data does not establish causation. Input-versus-productivity comparisons use only 30 districts.
  • District averages hide differences between farms.
  • Season C covers fewer crops and districts.
  • Predictions depend on data quality and on variables the survey did not collect.
  • Model estimates should support, not replace, expert decisions.
